01

What armature is

Software builders have everywhere to gather. Hardware builders have almost nowhere. armature is a permanent space where the people building atoms can work, show, and be seen.
→ 01

A workshop

Bench space, tools, and serious equipment to build on. A place to assemble, break, and iterate on a prototype without doing it alone in a garage.

→ 02

A showroom

Resident teams keep prototypes and demo material on the floor. Visiting investors, partners, and operators see working physical AI, not a pitch deck.

→ 03

A room

The lab is hosted. Founders, engineers, and backers who understand hardware share the space, so the hardest questions get answered by people who have been there.

→ 04

A training ground

Paid hands-on workshops, student research projects run with industry, and build days with the communities driving Indian robotics.

04

On-prem physical AI deployments

Your site, your data, your own physical AI. Continuous smart surveillance, safety monitoring, and local LLM use cases for hospitals, malls, hotels, factories, and institutes, running entirely on your premises.
→ 01

Continuous video cannot live in the cloud

Streaming dozens of cameras to the cloud around the clock is expensive and slow. A local GPU processes everything on site, in real time, at a fixed cost.

→ 02

Your footage never leaves the building

Hospitals, schools, and factories often cannot legally or practically send footage out. On-prem keeps every frame, and every LLM query, inside your walls.

→ 03

One box, many use cases

The same on-site GPU that watches the floor also runs your local assistant, your document search, and your site-specific models.

→ 04

Deployed and maintained by a lab, not a vendor

armature's engineers spec, install, and maintain the deployment, and the lab behind it means models keep improving after go-live.

03

Infrastructure stack

The robotics work depends on a local data and compute spine. These are the systems that let experiments repeat, scale, and stay private.
01 · Storage

Local NAS

TrueNAS or OpenMediaVault first: durable datasets, logs, CAD, checkpoints, and video.

02 · Compute

GPU node

A 2-GPU Autonomous Computer build before larger rack-scale experiments.

03 · Versioning

DVC remotes

Project-level dataset and model versioning on top of local storage.

04 · Object layer

S3 semantics

SeaweedFS or Garage only after the first NAS is stable and projects need object APIs.

05 · Operations

Open DC stack

NetBox, OpenBMC, SONiC, Metal3, and Ironic when the Lab has multiple nodes.

Pick a mission, bring a build log, and make the stack real. The Lab is where open-source robotics leaves the README.
