Chapter 01

Executive brief

This paper evaluates how Armature AI Labs could develop compact, serviceable robot actuators in India. The first objective is a well-characterised V0 joint with a competitive mass and package, followed by improvements in sustained operation, heat rejection and precision. Matching catalogue mass alone is not an equivalent actuator: output support, electronics, sensing, connectors and cooling must fit inside the same comparison boundary.

The intended arm remains a general-purpose manipulator for grasping, pick-and-place and object positioning. Laboratory work, test tubes, fine assembly, biological experiments, soldering and PCB handling are additional development goals. They are not validated capabilities. A dexterous hand is a separate actuator, sensing and software programme that also adds wrist mass and contact complexity.

Decision recommendation - begin with one instrumented medium rotary joint and a small wrist demonstrator. Preserve the existing RS-based reference study and compare Chinese actuators as engineering benchmarks. Pursue Indian design, assembly, testing and service; disclose imported components individually. Do not freeze a complete arm around unqualified catalogue torque.
  • Measure stationary holding and friction first. A joint may achieve a short peak torque yet be unsuitable for sustained gravity compensation or delicate contact.
  • Prioritise winding-to-housing heat flow, current/torque calibration, dual-encoder diagnostics, repairable construction and a documented thermal estimator before pursuing exotic motor concepts.
  • Treat OEM demonstrations as evidence of a robot task, not proof of undisclosed actuator life, thermal margins or supplier identity.
  • Localise processes in stages: housings, shafts, harnesses, winding and assembly are different industrial problems from precision gears, encoders and sintered rare-earth magnets.
  • Use the cost model to identify what quotations and yield data would change the decision. Its numbers are editable planning assumptions, not market prices.

How to review: engineering reviewers should challenge the load cases, thermal boundaries and test criteria; suppliers should correct specific capability or origin entries; business reviewers should replace cost assumptions with written quotations. Every referenced statement links to its source. Source IDs also resolve in the evidence appendix and spreadsheet. No supplier outreach or public publication was performed for this paper.

Chapter 03

The actuator as a complete system

A joint actuator is a controlled load path and a thermal system. A supplier may sell a bare motor, a gear unit, an integrated drive or a supported joint. Those are different procurement boundaries. The electronics, output bearing, encoder and brake can change mass and packaging as much as the motor choice. [E02] [E06] [E12] [E24]

Four different time claims. Warm-up time to a limit, sustainable operation at thermal equilibrium, duration/recovery of a short peak, and accumulated wear life are different questions. Running for eight hours at one light duty neither validates all loads nor establishes a thousands-of-hours service life. [E01] [E21] [E27]

Thermal limiting chain. Copper heating depends on current; magnetic losses rise with operating conditions including speed; the inverter contributes conduction/switching loss; gearbox/bearings/seals/lubricant dissipate mechanical losses. Windings, magnets, encoder, inverter, grease and touch surfaces can have different limits. [E01] [E02] [E11] [E14] [E17]

Holding is a separate operating point. At stationary output, mechanical output power is zero while energized torque can still generate copper and inverter heat. A running torque rating therefore does not automatically establish continuous zero-speed holding torque. Test multiple electrical rotor angles and the real mounting thermal path. [E01] [E11]

Precision and sensitivity. Backlash, elastic deflection, friction, cogging, energized ripple, encoder errors and thermal drift are distinct. Current-based torque estimation is not an actual output torque transducer; motor encoder resolution is not output positioning accuracy. [E04] [E06] [E13] [E18] [E26]

Installed mass and reflected inertia. For a simple reduction N = motor speed/output speed, rotor inertia referred to the output scales as N squared times rotor inertia; load inertia referred to the motor scales inversely. Distal mass also contributes gravity moment and link inertia. Comparisons should use a full robot model, not just module mass. [E03] [E02]

Chapter 05

Why heat, mass and precision interact

Thermal equilibrium and hours of operation

Engineering model: at zero speed, mechanical output power is zero even while the motor produces holding torque. Electrical and inverter losses can still be substantial. A simple thermal model is Cth dT/dt = Ploss - (T - Tamb)/Rth. For constant losses it approaches a steady rise of Ploss x Rth, with time constant Rth x Cth. More thermal mass delays heating; lower thermal resistance improves steady operation. These are different interventions. [E28]

Copper loss = sum over phases k of Rk(T) x Ik,rms^2. This becomes 3 x Rphase(T) x Iphase,rms^2 for balanced equal-phase RMS conditions. Thermal rise at equilibrium ≈ Ploss x Rth. Time constant = Rth x Cth.

These equations are an analytical starting point. Do not substitute DC bus current for phase RMS current, or phase-to-phase resistance for per-phase resistance without the winding connection and measurement convention. Motor torque constant conventions must match the current convention. Add inverter, iron, mechanical and gearbox losses for the tested operating point; temperature-dependent resistance creates feedback that a constant-loss model misses. [E01] [E11] [E17]

Illustrative calculation, not a component rating: with 30 W total heat and a 1.5 K/W system thermal resistance, steady temperature rise is 45 K. At 40 C ambient, the modelled node reaches 85 C. Halving thermal resistance lowers that node to 62.5 C. Doubling thermal capacitance only slows the rise. A winding hotspot may exceed that node, so a safe surface temperature does not establish winding or magnet safety.

Four distinct specifications are required: permissible continuous steady load; an intermittent peak envelope with recovery time; mission operating hours at a defined trajectory; and service life at defined loads and maintenance. A battery-runtime statement is not a joint-life statement. Eight hours of intermittent operation is not eight hours at peak torque. A short burn-in is not evidence for thousands of service hours. [E01] [E21] [E27]

Mass propagates upstream

Added shoulder gravity torque = sum(delta mass x g x horizontal offset). Added point-mass inertia = sum(delta mass x radius^2).

Illustrative calculation: adding 0.5 kg at 0.6 m horizontal offset adds 2.943 Nm of gravity torque and 0.18 kg m2 of point-mass inertia about that axis. At 5 rad/s2 it adds 0.9 Nm of acceleration torque in this simplified model. Adding the same mass near the shoulder axis contributes much less. Thus distal driver, brake, wrist and hand mass can trigger a larger shoulder actuator, which then increases structure and support requirements. [E03]

A complete arm calculation must include link distributed inertia, motor rotor inertia reflected through the square of gear ratio, joint geometry, payload centre of mass, counterbalances, contact loads and cable forces. The simple example is a sensitivity demonstration, not an arm payload rating. A light motor with a heavy external bearing or cooling plate may lose its apparent system advantage. [E02] [E03]

Accuracy is a chain, not an encoder resolution

An encoder quantises angle; it does not remove mechanical compliance, transmission error, friction, thermal drift or mounting error. A motor-side encoder cannot directly observe all output-side deflection or backlash. An output encoder improves observability but still needs calibration. A physical torque sensor measures strain or displacement through a calibrated load path; current-based torque estimation includes uncertain friction, ratio efficiency and temperature effects. [E13] [E18]

For delicate contact, test both directions near zero speed. Report breakaway torque, hysteresis, minimum repeatable controllable output torque, noise, bias drift and closed-loop stability. A high command rate is not the measured torque bandwidth; timestamps, filtering, transport, controller update and mechanics all contribute latency. Tool-level force sensitivity also depends on kinematics and the tool contact geometry. [E03] [E04] [E18]

Speed, energy and wear

Mechanical power = torque x angular speed. Linear power = force x velocity. Screw motoring torque ≈ force x lead / (2 pi x forward efficiency). Lowering/backdriving needs reverse-efficiency and friction analysis; self-locking must be checked.

For illustration, 20 Nm at 40 rpm is about 83.8 W of mechanical output. A 2 kN linear force at 0.1 m/s is 200 W. These are not electrical-input ratings. High torque and maximum no-load speed must never be multiplied as if simultaneously available. Back-EMF, bus voltage, current limits, winding temperature and transmission speed limits shape the envelope. [E01]

Deceleration and lowering a load can return energy to the DC bus. Specify how much the battery or power supply can absorb, and design bus clamps, braking resistors or a coordinated motion strategy where necessary. A bench supply that sources current may not sink it. Power-loss tests must distinguish motor shutdown, controlled stop, brake engagement and stored elastic/gravitational energy. [E12]

Transmission wear, bearing preload, lubrication and sealing can change friction and accuracy before a joint fails completely. Endurance acceptance should include drift in lost motion, efficiency, temperature rise, vibration and noise, not merely continued rotation. An accelerated test is credible only when it preserves the relevant failure mechanism. [E14] [E27]

More thermal mass can delay a temperature rise without improving the eventual sustainable load. Improved steady cooling requires a better path to the environment. A heat pipe moves heat; it still needs an effective condenser/sink. The published heat-pipe experiment varies orientation and airflow, which are especially relevant when a robot joint changes posture. A phase-change insert should be rated by finite absorbed energy and its reset time, not advertised as indefinite cooling. The phase-change proposal and its test gate are engineering recommendations. [E28] [E30]

The limiting temperature can belong to the gearbox lubricant, encoder, drive electronics or exposed surface instead of the motor insulation. Kollmorgen explicitly discusses neighboring component limits in frameless robotic joints. Use the actual selected parts and their mounting conditions; do not reuse the example temperatures from that application note as universal design limits. A proposed component temperature register should list normal limit, protection threshold, measurement location, uncertainty and required margin for each part. [E02]

Chapter 06

Four application classes: provisional requirements

All numerical values below are proposed study targets for trade-off and fixture design, not established actuator specifications or final Armature product requirements. Each torque or force point must be paired with speed, duration, ambient, mounting and cooldown. Targets may be revised after the first measured joint.

The classes prevent a low-friction wrist from being judged by the same priorities as an impact-loaded knee. For wrists and hands, moving mass and contact sensitivity are central. Shoulders need sustained gravity support; hips and knees need energy handling and impact tolerance; ankles combine support and contact transitions. Linear actuators must be evaluated through the linkage moment arm, including near-singular positions.

A proposed development screen is an eight-hour mission at stated ambient, with repeated motion/hold/idle segments and logged sensor data. Independently test stationary holding to equilibrium. A provisional near-steady screening criterion is less than 1 C total drift over the final 30 minutes at all measured winding, housing and driver nodes. Even this does not prove equilibrium: use a fitted thermal model and sufficient settling time, with predicted remaining rise below a declared margin. Stop at any predeclared component limit. Passing a particular mission does not qualify arbitrary trajectories.

A small parallel-gripper tool should precede a dexterous hand. The common interface needs a mechanically indexed datum, positive retention, defined electrical power and communications, tool identity and mass/centre-of-mass metadata. Repeatable attachment does not itself prove tool-position accuracy; check deflection and calibration after repeated changes. A wrist force/torque sensor, compliant fixture or remote-centre compliance may matter more for assembly than increasing peak motor torque.

For soldering, biological work and sensitive electronics, actuator development is only one layer. Task-specific fixturing, sensing, thermal isolation, contamination control, cable management and process verification need independent acceptance. Avoid converting a successful general pick-and-place demonstration into a claim of reliable biological or electrical work.

Recommended investment order: fund measurements and fixtures first; then one repairable V0 module and the most promising thermal-path change; then repeatability and endurance; finally production tooling. Defer expensive topology changes until measured loss and failure budgets show that conventional improvements cannot meet the target. The white paper supplies a decision framework, not a guarantee that a specific unbuilt actuator will succeed.
